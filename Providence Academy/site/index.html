παιδεία · paideia

Paideia was the Greek word for the complete formation of a person — intellect, character, and taste shaped together into someone fit for civic and moral life.

It is the word behind pedagogy, encyclopedia, and pediatric. It assumed that education produces a kind of person, and that the only real question is which kind. We are not inventing this. We are returning to it.

The seven virtues, and how each one is taught.

Every school forms character through what it rewards, what it repeats, and what it leaves out. Ours is written down.

About the Academy

Recovering an older idea of school.

Providence Home Academy exists because education makes a kind of person, and we think a parent deserves to know which kind. The classical tradition answered that question openly for two thousand years. It taught Latin, because a child who can parse a sentence can parse an argument. It taught history in a four-year cycle, so that the same story returns three times and lands deeper each time. It taught memory work, because a mind furnished with good things has something to think with. And it taught the seven virtues by name, in the plain expectation that character is formed on purpose or by accident.

We have put that tradition into a form a family can actually run at the kitchen table. Every lesson is open to read before a child is enrolled. What we are aiming at is a young person who reasons carefully, writes clearly, keeps his word, and can still be moved by a poem.

Pacing

No child waits. No record overstates.

Each subject moves at the speed the child actually reads, writes and reasons. A strong arithmetic student is not held to the pace of her history, and a hard week in one subject does not slow the others.

The record stays honest about it. A grade level is certified only when every required subject for that level is finished — the slowest one, not the average.

Georgia asks for 180 days of 4½ hours. The last day works itself out from the first day, the holidays and your breaks.
Three kinds of days off
Federal holidays are built in. Turn any of them into a school day.
Breaks are your own, planned ahead, as many as you like. Each one moves the last day later, so the 180 days look after themselves.
Sick and travel days are unplanned. Mark them on the day from Teaching. Five a term.
On any day off, teaching still counts. It adds to the streak and to the attendance log.

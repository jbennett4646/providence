παιδεία · paideia

An Older Idea of School

Paideia was the Greek word for the complete formation of a person: intellect, character and taste, shaped together.

Latin, a four-year history cycle, memory work and the seven virtues by name, put into a form a family can run at the kitchen table.

Trivium · Quadrivium · Progymnasmata

The Classical Path

Old names for a simple idea: teach each thing when a child is ready for it.

Trivium the three ways

Words, in the order children grow. First the facts, then how to reason about them, then how to say it well.

Grammar · Logic · Rhetoric
Quadrivium the four ways

Number, studied in the world. Counting, then shape and space, then pattern and harmony, then the movement of the heavens.

Arithmetic · Geometry · Music · Astronomy
Progymnasmata first exercises

How the ancients taught writing: short assignments that build one skill at a time, from retelling a fable to making an argument.

The Seven Virtues

I Prudence Logic, and stating the other side’s case before answering it.
II Justice History read from sources, so judgment rests on what people did.
III Fortitude Hard subjects kept hard. Latin is not made easier to be kind.
IV Temperance A daily rhythm, finished work, and a school day that ends.
V Faith Sacred history read whole, in its own words.
VI Hope Psalms, poems and speeches carried into adult life.
VII Charity Recitation to a listener, because clear speech is for another person.
Character is formed on purpose or by accident.

The Seven Virtues
Character is formed on purpose or by accident. Here is how each virtue is taught.
I
Prudence
Logic, and stating the other side’s case before answering it.
II
Justice
History read from sources, so judgment rests on what people did.
III
Fortitude
Hard subjects kept hard. Latin is not made easier to be kind.
IV
Temperance
A daily rhythm, finished work, and a school day that ends.
V
Faith
Sacred history read whole, in its own words.
VI
Hope
Psalms, poems and speeches carried into adult life.
VII
Charity
Recitation to a listener, because clear speech is for another person.

The Classical Path
Old names for a simple idea: teach each thing when a child is ready for it.
Triviumthe three ways
Words, in the order children grow. First they learn the facts, then how to reason about them, then how to say it well.
Grammar · Logic · Rhetoric
Quadriviumthe four ways
Number, studied in the world. Counting, then shape and space, then pattern and harmony, then the movement of the heavens.
Arithmetic · Geometry · Music · Astronomy
Progymnasmatafirst exercises
How the ancients taught writing: short assignments that build one skill at a time, from retelling a fable to making an argument.
Fable · Narrative · … · Argument

A Day at the Table
Every day · about 40 minutes Short daily practice Scripture, recitation, spelling and drills. It’s what keeps facts from fading, and on a sick day it’s all that’s asked.
This week · your schedule The week’s lessons Grammar, history, science, literature and the rest. Any day, any order. Finish early, double up, or save one for Saturday.
On screen or on paper Keep the screen away if you like. Print the year plan once and each term’s manual as you reach it. A one-tap weekly check-in keeps the records current. Switch any time.
